# Roadmap from verified beta to full game

This roadmap preserves the entire intended game. It is not a promise of a delivery date, team size, budget, or unlimited engine capacity. Complete a stage's behavior and evidence before moving to the next. Expand content after proving its underlying system, not by adding empty menus for future features.

## F1 — Stabilize the released beta

Exit conditions: packaged campaign is recoverable after setbacks; the economy and military ledger remain consistent through long simulations; AI can sustain itself without cheating; new players understand growth and losses; formations can navigate gates/bridges without widespread deadlock; save migration policy is operating. Fix exploits in repeated gifts, infinite imports, demobilization, repair, artifact duplication, and reinforcements before adding more regions.

Retain the original architecture when it works. Replace a component only after profiling or measured quality problems identify it, with a regression suite and a saved-game migration path.

## F2 — Expand Japan as content, not a new campaign engine

Add researched region packs to the same node/route model. The full playable geography covers Japan at the selected historical scope, with documented regional boundaries and period names. Do not backfill a modern political map as an 1180 one. Additional settlements remain aggregated when off-screen; only the inspected city needs full presentation.

New routes receive ordered tactical sites, regional terrain variants, local intelligence conditions, and valid reinforcement approaches. Important crossings and coastal transitions must agree with geography. Historical transport frameworks inform authoring, but do not imply this game includes an exact reconstructed road network.

Exit conditions: add a region without changing core route code; region validation catches impossible or duplicate connections; save/load works across expanded content; campaign AI handles fronts and distant allies without using hidden state. Clearly separate mainland geography, peripheral regions, and future optional expansions where historical polity boundaries are not identical to the modern state.

## F3 — Cities, institutions, and location-specific excellence

Expand automatic plot generation, district identity, delegated governance, trade, and culture. Add stable district split/merge with genealogies so a soldier's historical origin remains identifiable after boundaries change. Develop rebuilding, displacement, pilgrimage networks, multiple learning traditions, mature ports, specialized agrarian regions, and resource/cultural opportunities.

Each "bonus district" requires geographic or institutional conditions, people, inputs, and sustained development. A port's maritime advantage comes from useful harbors and skilled communities, not a universal naval-damage percentage. A master smithing region can depend on imported iron but still needs fuel, labor, craft continuity, and transport.

Religion remains overlapping institutions and practices, not a mandatory binary state-religion choice. Cultural power attracts opportunities and recognition but cannot manufacture consent or material resources without costs. Institutions can provide relief or organize resistance depending on treatment and interests.

Exit conditions: several viable city trajectories; a small culturally prestigious power can matter; towns are visually distinct without rigid zoning; persistent old centers survive growth; no dominant build order universally wins.

## F4 — Complete craft and elite progression

Deepen smithing into specialist production capabilities, quality assurance, regional knowledge traditions, repair, and trade in excellent equipment. Extend the existing skill/mentor/capacity model to shipwrights, builders, navigators, and scholars. Preserve the distinction between one great master and a high-throughput industry.

Expand elite samurai paths around mounted archery, disciplined foot combat, exceptional armor/weapons, cultural/martial standing, and experimental technologies. Maintain simple useful ordinary troop roles. Districts support different elite traditions through investment, not immutable class stereotypes. Military training is distinct from civilian occupation.

Add carefully researched optional champion demonstrations and negotiated challenges using the event system. They influence reputation/morale and limited disputes; they are not a generic duel-for-every-city solution. No need for a per-samurai family/RPG interface.

Exit conditions: elite quality visibly depends on sustained training and craft inputs; losing skilled troops creates lasting regional consequences; ordinary troops retain counterplay; advanced units do not make positioning irrelevant; scarce master skills cannot be restored instantly with money.

## F5 — Large tactical warfare and fortification maturity

Scale beyond the beta's measured load in explicit stages: 12,000, 20,000, then 40,000 total soldiers as test fixtures before choosing final shipped ceilings. Larger targets, including 60,000-plus combined participants, remain stretch goals until measured. Keep small individual formations. Group templates, line orders, readable reserves, and filtering must scale with formation count.

Profile terrain navigation, congestion, contact calculations, animation, visual crowding, projectile representation, corpses, CPU/GPU transfer, and UI selection. More efficient rendering alone does not solve command bandwidth or pathfinding. If representation backend changes, preserve the authoritative soldier registry and battle outcomes.

Expand manual/auto fortification modules, period-appropriate materials, multi-stage compounds, siege works, and better defensive AI. The constructed layout remains the assault map. Introduce complex castle geometry only with pathing and balance tests that prevent impassable wall mazes or unavoidable kill corridors.

Exit conditions: declared maximum scale works in a packaged battle with active combat and camera control on a published tested hardware tier; no silent unit inflation or removed command access; late-game defenses offer assault, blockade, negotiation, and strategic alternatives.

## F6 — Foreign contact and artifact-driven alternate development

Expand off-map contacts first: domestic gateways, continental contacts, and further trade-network relays. A distant artifact can arrive through intermediaries without requiring a Japanese ship to reach its original country. Track source, travel time, reliability, cost, and access. No need to render the whole globe to support long-distance trade.

Each artifact has a documented historical availability profile or a clearly speculative one. High player capability can accelerate encounter, comprehension, reproduction, and innovation. It cannot import an object that exists nowhere yet unless the scenario explicitly posits alternate foreign development. A locally invented breakthrough is a different event with its own prerequisites, not an unexplained future import.

Retain specimen/text/artisan acquisition, interpretation, prototype, validation, production, and training stages. Elite-only gunpowder eligibility remains the intended main ruleset unless the user explicitly changes it. Advanced technology uses abstract game resources and prerequisites, not real manufacturing recipes.

Exit conditions: different development paths work; lost institutions reduce capability without deleting already-made artifacts; foreign access matters without becoming the only winning strategy; advanced technology creates logistical/training costs and tactical counters; historical and speculative content are visibly separated.

## F7 — Regional politics, conquest, and fragile coalitions

Expand relationship memory, institutional negotiation, dispute mediation, tribute, local autonomy, vassal obligations where appropriate, surrender terms, and coalition leadership. Collective interests remain primary. A limited roster of generals can develop and age, but no individual samurai relationship web is required.

Add more occupation histories and district responses. Large cities should challenge governance through capacity and competing institutions, not only a bigger fixed rebellion penalty. Losses, promises, exposed covert activity, siege suffering, and relief retain evidence. Religious institutions may help or resist; neither is automatic.

Coalitions can form readily around security, cultural respect, or shared threats, and can fracture over disproportionate burdens, deaths, unrewarded contributions, ignored needs, and broken promises. Members remain strategically useful autonomous actors; they are neither disposable army sources nor constantly random betrayers.

Exit conditions: direct conquest and culturally supported coalition leadership both work; smaller allies matter; victory cannot be bought through trivial gift spam; player restraint has measurable advantages without making coercion mechanically impossible; recovery after a coalition fracture is playable.

## F8 — Maritime networks as a complete operational system

Expand ports, merchant networks, navigation, ship construction and repairs, crew careers, escort roles, transport, weather risks, blockades, coastal intelligence, and regional sea encounters. Continue using the naval encounter simulation and transparent narratives. A tactical naval RTS remains a separate optional product decision, not a full-game completion requirement.

Exit conditions: maritime craftsmanship and merchant competence change outcomes through explicit causes; coast-specific districts matter; sea logistics create genuine options around a blocked land route; crew casualties and ship losses feed back to the correct districts; naval and land clocks cannot be exploited.

## F9 — Imperial endgame and campaign continuity

Implement the user's alternate-history Emperor victory. Recognized coalition leadership is a reachable intermediate achievement. Imperial recognition requires several durable supports: broad political consent or enforced settlement, exceptional legitimacy/culture, viable administration, secure access to the imperial center, resolved major rival claims, and enough economic/military resilience to sustain the new order.

Allow a militarily dominant path and a culturally prestigious coalition path. A small faction must first earn and maintain enough recognition; high culture alone cannot instantly grant every ally's sovereignty. Control of Kyoto is relevant but not an automatic coronation trigger. Present the outcome as alternate history, distinct from ordinary historical shogunal rule.

Provide a ratification/crisis phase with opposition, coalition demands, institutional conditions, and a sustained recognition period. After success, offer continuation in a changed political environment rather than deleting the simulation. Historical campaigns may provide an optional recognized military-government endpoint, but it must not silently replace the requested emperor goal.

Exit conditions: both primary paths can win in independent playtests; imperial authority cannot be claimed while key requirements are temporarily faked; defeated claimants and broken coalitions are resolved coherently; save continuity survives the transition.

## F10 — Release completion, not perpetual feature expansion

Full release needs content completeness at the declared era/region scope, onboarding, difficulty settings, accessibility, historical/alternate labels, asset licensing, reliable saves, packaged performance, long-run stability, and documented supported hardware. All beta and expanded invariants remain passing. Final review should reject a system that exists only as a development console or UI mock.

Do not add multiplayer, a global explorable map, personal dynasty relationships, or unrestricted architectural CAD to finish this vision. Those would be new projects. Set a final content freeze and complete usability and balance rather than continually creating new branches of design.
